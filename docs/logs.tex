\section*{Key Empirical Insights}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{High Fidelity on Standard Baselines:} IndicConformer-600M achieves its strongest accuracy on standard reference dialects, such as Standard Pune Marathi D3 (10.87\% RNNT WER) and Standard Khariboli Hindi D3 (9.15\% RNNT WER).
  \item \textbf{Severe Sub-Dialect Degradation Gap:} When presented with regional sub-dialects, error rates increase significantly. For example, South Konkan Marathi (D1) exhibits a \textbf{+23.50\% absolute WER increase} over Standard Pune D3, while Rayalaseema Telugu (D2) exhibits a \textbf{+7.31\% absolute WER gap} over Coastal Andhra D1.
  \item \textbf{Under-Resourced Regional Language Challenge:} Languages such as Chhattisgarhi (\textbf{58.28\% RNNT WER}) and Maithili (\textbf{48.64\% RNNT WER}) suffer from heavy phonetic and morphological divergence when evaluated using generic Hindi model conditioning, illustrating the necessity of dedicated text normalisation.
  \item \textbf{Decoder Architecture Comparison:} Across all 9 languages, the autoregressive RNN-T decoder consistently outperforms the CTC decoder by an average of \textbf{0.51\% absolute WER}, providing superior character-level predictions and token alignment.
  \item \textbf{Justification for Post-ASR Text-to-Text Normaliser:} The high substitution and insertion rates across sub-dialects demonstrate that acoustic model errors are primarily lexical and orthographic, strongly validating our proposed \textbf{Text-to-Text Normalisation Model} ($\text{Dialect Text} \rightarrow \text{Standard Formal Text}$).
\end{enumerate}
